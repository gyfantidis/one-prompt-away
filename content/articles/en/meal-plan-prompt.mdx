Every Sunday evening, the same story: you open the fridge, have no idea what to cook, and end up ordering delivery. Sound familiar? It happened to me hundreds of times.

Until I tried one prompt.

## The Problem

I want a meal plan for the week, but:
- No time to search for recipes
- Want to eat healthy without overthinking it
- Need a shopping list, not just ideas

## The Prompt

Here's the prompt I use — copy-paste ready:

```prompt
Create a weekly meal plan (Monday–Friday) for 2 people.

Rules:
- Mediterranean diet
- Budget: max €50 per week
- Cooking time: max 30 minutes per meal
- Lunch and dinner only
- Use overlapping ingredients (if I buy chicken, use it in 2+ meals)

Output format:
1. Meal plan table (day | lunch | dinner)
2. Shopping list divided by category (vegetables, proteins, dairy, other)
3. Estimated cost per category
```

## The Result

In 28 seconds I had:
- Complete 5-day meal plan
- 23 items on the shopping list
- Estimated cost: €47.50
- Bonus: prep tips for Sunday (batch cooking suggestions)

The key was the "overlapping ingredients" rule — instead of buying 15 different things, the AI designed meals that share ingredients. Buy one whole chicken, make souvlaki on Monday and soup on Wednesday.

## Why It Works

Three things make this prompt effective:

First, **it gives constraints**. Without a budget and time limit, AI gives you generic suggestions. With constraints, you get an actionable plan.

Second, **it defines the output format**. You don't want a paragraph of text — you want a table and a list. Tell it what format you need.

Third, **it thinks practically**. The "overlapping ingredients" rule is a meta-constraint that makes the whole plan more realistic.

## Takeaway

Next time you think "what should I cook", don't search for recipes. Take this prompt, customize the constraints (budget, diet, number of people), and hit Enter.

30 seconds. That's all that separates you.
