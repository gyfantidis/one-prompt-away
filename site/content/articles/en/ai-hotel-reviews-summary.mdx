## The Problem With Hotel Reviews

Last week I was booking a hotel for a trip to Thessaloniki. The place looked great in photos, had 4.2 stars, and seemed reasonably priced.

Then I made the mistake of reading the reviews.

200+ reviews. Some loved it. Some hated it. One person complained about noise, another said it was peaceful. Someone mentioned "outdated bathroom," while another praised the "renovated rooms."

After 20 minutes of scrolling, I was more confused than when I started.

So I let AI do the reading for me.

## What I Did

I copied about 50 recent reviews from Booking.com (you can do this from Google Maps, TripAdvisor, or any review site). Then I pasted them into ChatGPT with a specific prompt.

Here's the exact prompt I used:

prompt
I'm considering booking this hotel. Below are real guest reviews.

Analyze them and tell me:
1. The 3 most common complaints (with how many people mentioned each)
2. The 3 most praised things (with how many people mentioned each)
3. One sentence: Should I book this hotel for a quiet weekend trip? Yes or No, and why.

Reviews:
[paste reviews here]


## The Result

In about 10 seconds, ChatGPT gave me this:

**Most common complaints:**
- Street noise at night (mentioned by 12 people)
- Slow elevator (mentioned by 8 people)
- Breakfast variety could be better (mentioned by 6 people)

**Most praised things:**
- Friendly and helpful staff (mentioned by 23 people)
- Perfect central location (mentioned by 19 people)
- Clean rooms (mentioned by 15 people)

**Verdict:** No. For a quiet weekend, the street noise complaints are a red flag. 12 out of 50 reviewers mentioned it specifically. Consider asking for a room facing the courtyard, or look for a quieter alternative.

That's it. Three lines that saved me from potentially ruining my weekend.

## Why This Works Better Than Star Ratings

Star ratings are basically useless for decision-making. A 4.2 tells you nothing about *your* specific needs.

Maybe you don't care about breakfast because you eat out. Maybe you're a heavy sleeper and noise doesn't bother you. Maybe you need wheelchair access and that's mentioned in exactly 2 reviews buried on page 7.

The AI finds patterns that matter to *your* question.

## How to Copy Reviews (The Easy Way)

On most review sites:
1. Scroll down to load more reviews (the more, the better)
2. Select the text with your mouse — just drag from the first review to the last
3. Copy (Ctrl+C on computer, long-press and "Copy" on phone)
4. Paste into ChatGPT

Don't worry about formatting. ChatGPT handles messy text just fine.

If you're on your phone, 20-30 reviews is enough to spot patterns. On desktop, go for 50+ if you have time.

## Other Ways I've Used This

Once I realized how well this works, I started using it everywhere:

**For restaurants:** "Is this place good for a business lunch? Quiet enough for conversation?"

**For products on Amazon:** "I need wireless earbuds for running. Do these stay in place during exercise?"

**For apps:** "I want a budgeting app. Is this one actually easy to use or do people give up after a week?"

The key is asking a specific question about *your* situation, not just "is this good."

## A More Advanced Prompt

If you want even more detail, try this version:

prompt
Analyze these reviews for a [type of trip/purchase].

I specifically care about:
- [thing 1]
- [thing 2]
- [thing 3]

For each thing I care about, tell me what reviewers say. Quote specific reviews when relevant.

Then give me a final recommendation in 2 sentences.

Reviews:
[paste here]


For my Thessaloniki hotel, I could have added: "I specifically care about: noise levels, WiFi quality, walking distance to the waterfront."

## The Takeaway

You don't need to read 200 reviews anymore. Copy them, paste them into ChatGPT, and ask a specific question about your needs. You'll get a clear answer in seconds — not a star rating, but actual insight.

Next time you're stuck in review paralysis, let AI do the reading. Your eyes (and your vacation) will thank you.
