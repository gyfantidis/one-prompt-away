## Why My Emails Used to Take 30 Minutes

Before I found these prompts, every email was a struggle. I'd stare at a blank page, write, delete, rewrite. Now I finish in 3 minutes.

I tested dozens of prompts. These 5 are the only ones that work every single time.

## 1. Cold Outreach That Doesn't Feel Cold

```prompt
Write a cold email to [NAME] who works as [TITLE] at [COMPANY]. I want to [GOAL — e.g. book a meeting / propose a collaboration].

Tone: Friendly but professional
Length: Max 80 words
Include: One specific compliment about their company
Start: Without "I hope you're well" — get straight to the point
```

Why it works: It doesn't sound like a template. The specific compliment shows you did your research.

## 2. Follow-Up Email Without Looking Desperate

```prompt
I sent an email [TIME AGO] about [TOPIC]. I haven't heard back. Write a follow-up email that:

- Doesn't mention that they didn't reply
- Adds new value (tip, resource, insight)
- Gives an easy out if they're not interested
- Max 60 words

Tone: Chill, not pushy
```

The magic: The "adds new value" instruction makes the email useful, not annoying.

## 3. Complaint Email That Gets Action

```prompt
I have a problem with [DESCRIPTION]. Write an email to customer service that:

- Describes the issue objectively (no emotions)
- Mentions what I've already tried
- Asks for a specific solution
- Sets a reasonable deadline
- Tone: Stern but polite

Start with: "I need your help resolving..."
```

Works like magic: Companies take emails seriously when they look organized.

## 4. Thank You Email That Won't Be Forgotten

```prompt
I want to thank [NAME] for [WHAT THEY DID]. Write a thank you email that:

- Is specific (not generic "thanks for everything")
- Mentions how what they did helped me
- Offers something back (connection, resource, favor)
- Max 70 words
- Tone: Genuine gratitude
```

Pro tip: The "offers something back" part makes all the difference. It shows you're not just taking.

## 5. Rejection Email That Keeps Doors Open

```prompt
I want to say no to [REQUEST/PROPOSAL] from [NAME]. Write an email that:

- Says no clearly but kindly
- Briefly explains why (without over-justifying)
- Suggests an alternative if there is one
- Leaves the door open for the future
- Max 65 words
```

Why it works: People appreciate honesty. And they remember when you treat them with respect.

## The Secret Sauce

The key isn't the prompts — it's the specifics you put inside them. The more detailed the input, the better the output.

**Pro tip**: Keep these prompts in a note app. Copy-paste and only change the [BRACKETS].

Start with the first prompt today. You'll see the difference immediately.
