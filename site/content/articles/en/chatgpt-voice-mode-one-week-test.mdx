## The Experiment

I type a lot. Emails, brainstorms, quick questions to ChatGPT — my fingers are constantly on the keyboard. Last week, I decided to change that completely.

For seven days, I used ChatGPT's voice mode for everything. No typing. Just talking.

Voice mode is a feature in the ChatGPT mobile app that lets you have a spoken conversation with the AI. You talk, it listens, processes, and responds out loud. Like a phone call, but with an AI.

## How to Start Using Voice Mode

It's simple. Open the ChatGPT app on your phone. Tap the headphone icon at the bottom right corner. Start talking.

That's it. No settings to change, no subscriptions needed for basic use. The free tier includes voice mode, though the paid version gives you more advanced voice options and longer conversations.

## Day 1-2: The Awkward Phase

I felt ridiculous at first. Talking to my phone while walking to grab coffee. Asking ChatGPT to help me draft an email while cooking dinner.

But something clicked quickly. I was getting things done while my hands were busy. I asked it to brainstorm newsletter topics while folding laundry. I had it explain a concept while I was stuck in traffic.

The AI responds naturally. It pauses when you pause. It asks clarifying questions. It doesn't feel like dictation — it feels like conversation.

## Day 3-4: Finding the Sweet Spots

Some tasks work brilliantly with voice:

**Brainstorming.** I'd say something like:

prompt
I need five ideas for Instagram posts about coffee shops. Make them casual and fun.


And it would fire back options immediately. I could say "I like the second one, make it shorter" without touching my phone.

**Learning new things.** I asked it to explain how cryptocurrency works while on a walk. It broke it down step by step, and I could interrupt with "wait, what does that mean?" whenever I got lost.

**Quick tasks.** "Write a polite way to decline a meeting invitation" took five seconds to ask and ten seconds to get a usable response.

## Day 5-6: The Limitations

Voice mode isn't perfect.

**Complex formatting doesn't work well.** When I asked for a table or a bulleted list, hearing it read aloud was confusing. I had to switch to text for anything visual.

**Privacy matters.** I couldn't use it in quiet offices or crowded trains. Some conversations need discretion.

**Long outputs are hard to follow.** If I asked for a 500-word article, listening to the whole thing felt tedious. For longer content, typing and reading is still faster.

## Day 7: The Verdict

By the end of the week, I'd developed a rhythm. Voice for quick tasks, brainstorms, and learning. Text for complex work, formatting, and anything over a few paragraphs.

The biggest surprise? I actually thought more clearly when speaking. Typing makes me edit as I go. Speaking forced me to think through what I actually wanted before asking.

I found myself being more direct with my prompts. Instead of typing out elaborate instructions, I'd just say:

prompt
I'm writing an email to a client who missed a deadline. I need to be firm but not rude. Help me phrase it.


Natural language. Like explaining a problem to a colleague.

## Three Tips If You Try This

**Start with simple asks.** Quick questions, short brainstorms, definitions. Build comfort before attempting complex tasks.

**Use it when your hands are busy.** Commuting, cooking, exercising. That's where voice mode shines.

**Switch modes freely.** You can start in voice and continue in text. The conversation history stays intact.

## The Bottom Line

Voice mode won't replace typing for everyone. But for quick interactions, learning, and brainstorming on the go, it's genuinely useful.

I'm keeping it as part of my toolkit. About 30% of my ChatGPT interactions are now voice-based.

**Try it today:** Open the ChatGPT app, tap the headphone icon, and ask it one simple question. See how it feels. That's all it takes to start.
