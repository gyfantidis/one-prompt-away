Last month I had a pitch meeting scheduled. Problem: I had the idea but zero documentation. No business plan, no financial projections, no competitive analysis. The meeting was in 5 days.

I decided to test whether AI could actually help me build something investor-ready. Not a generic template. A real, specific business plan for my actual startup idea.

Four hours later, I had 47 pages. The investor's first comment? "This is thorough. Who helped you write it?"

Here's exactly how I did it.

## The Setup: Context Is Everything

Most people fail at business plan prompts because they ask for "a business plan." That's like asking someone to build you a house without mentioning where, how big, or what style.

I started with a massive context dump. One prompt, all the details.

prompt
I'm building a B2B SaaS product for local restaurants. The product helps them manage online orders from multiple delivery platforms (Wolt, efood, UberEats) in one dashboard. Target market is Greece, specifically restaurants doing 50-200 orders/day. Pricing model is €49/month base + €0.10 per order above 500. I have €15,000 to start. My background is in hospitality tech. Main competitors are Otter and Deliverect but they don't serve the Greek market well.

I need to create a complete business plan for a seed investor meeting. Keep this context for all our conversations today.


This prompt does nothing by itself. But it sets the stage for everything else.

## Phase 1: Market Analysis (45 minutes)

I asked for research I could actually verify.

prompt
Create a market analysis section for my business plan. Include:
1. TAM/SAM/SOM calculations for Greece (show your math)
2. Key market trends in food delivery for 2024-2025
3. Regulatory factors affecting restaurant tech in EU
4. 5 specific data points I should verify from industry reports

Format as a business plan section with headers.


The output gave me structure. But here's the key: I spent 20 minutes fact-checking the numbers. AI got the trends right but overestimated the market size by about 30%. I corrected it.

## Phase 2: Competitive Analysis (30 minutes)

This is where AI really shines. It can process competitor information faster than I ever could.

prompt
Analyze these 3 competitors for my business plan: Otter, Deliverect, Cuboh.

For each one, create a table with:
- Pricing (find their actual pricing pages)
- Key features
- Geographic focus
- Weaknesses based on G2/Capterra reviews
- Why they don't fit the Greek market specifically

End with a positioning statement for my product.


I had to feed it some review snippets manually since it couldn't access G2 directly. But the analysis framework saved me hours.

## Phase 3: Financial Projections (60 minutes)

This was the hardest part. I needed realistic numbers, not fantasy hockey-stick growth.

prompt
Create 3-year financial projections for my restaurant SaaS. Use these assumptions:
- Month 1: 5 restaurants (friends/family)
- Customer acquisition cost: €150
- Monthly churn: 5%
- Average order volume per restaurant: 120/day
- My monthly burn rate: €3,000 (solo founder)

Show monthly projections for Year 1, quarterly for Years 2-3. Include revenue, costs, runway calculation. Be conservative — I'd rather under-promise.


The model created a spreadsheet-ready breakdown. I imported it into Google Sheets and adjusted assumptions twice until the numbers felt honest.

## Phase 4: The Pitch Narrative (45 minutes)

Numbers alone don't win funding. Story does.

prompt
Write an executive summary (max 400 words) for my business plan. Structure:

1. The problem (2-3 sentences, specific pain point)
2. The solution (what we do, not how)
3. Why now (market timing)
4. Why me (founder credibility)
5. The ask (€50,000 for 12 months runway, specific milestones)

Tone: confident but not arrogant. Use one specific anecdote about a restaurant owner's frustration.


I rewrote the opening paragraph myself. AI gave me structure; I added personality.

## The Result

Total time: 4 hours and 12 minutes. Final document: 47 pages including appendices.

The investor asked detailed questions about my churn assumptions and competitive moat. He never asked if I used AI. When I told him afterward, he shrugged: "Doesn't matter. The thinking is solid."

I didn't get funded that round. But I got a second meeting and specific feedback. The business plan wasn't the problem.

## What Actually Matters

AI won't write your business plan. It'll help you organize your thinking faster than ever before.

The prompts only work if you:
- Know your business deeply
- Fact-check every claim
- Rewrite anything that sounds generic
- Treat AI output as a first draft, never final

The 4 hours I spent? Probably 60% was editing and verifying. The AI saved me from the blank page. That's its real value.

**Takeaway:** Start with a context prompt that explains your business completely. Then ask for specific sections with specific formats. Verify everything. Your business plan is only as good as your inputs.
