I wrote a LinkedIn post quickly one morning. I wasn't expecting anything. I woke up the next day to 47,000 impressions.

It wasn't luck — it was structure.

## Why Most LinkedIn Posts Fail

They're written for the person writing them, not the person reading them. They start with "Excited to announce..." or "Today I learned...". The feed is full of these. Nobody stops to read them.

Viral content starts with a hook that makes the reader think: *this is about me*.

## The Prompt

```prompt
Write a LinkedIn post about [TOPIC].

Hook: One counter-intuitive observation or contradiction that makes the reader stop
Body: 3-4 bullet points or short paragraphs with actionable insights
CTA: A question that invites comments

Tone: Direct, first person, no corporate speak
Length: Max 200 words
```

That simple. Three lines of instructions that change everything.

## Why This Structure Works

**The hook is everything.** LinkedIn only shows the first 2-3 lines before "...see more". If those don't pull people in, the post is dead. A counter-intuitive observation works because our brains automatically stop when they see something that contradicts expectations.

**Bullet points hold attention.** Text blocks get skipped. Bullets get read.

**The question brings comments.** And comments bring reach. LinkedIn's algorithm favors posts with engagement in the first 60 minutes.

## Example

I used the prompt with the topic "mistakes freelancers make with pricing".

The hook I got:
> *If you charge hourly, you're being paid to be slow.*

Did you stop to read that? That's exactly what a good hook does.

## Takeaway

Next time you want to write a LinkedIn post, don't write directly. Take this prompt, add your topic, and let the AI do the first draft. You edit, add your voice, and publish.

The post takes 5 minutes to write. The hook is what makes the difference.
