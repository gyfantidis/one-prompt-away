## The Problem: Interview Anxiety Was Real

I had a job interview scheduled for a marketing coordinator position. My last interview was three years ago, and I was terrified. I kept thinking about all the questions they might ask and drawing blanks.

Then I thought — what if I could practice with someone who never gets tired of my stumbling answers? That's when I decided to try mock interviews with ChatGPT.

## What a Mock Interview with AI Looks Like

A mock interview is basically a practice run. You simulate the real thing so you're not caught off guard. The AI plays the interviewer, asks you realistic questions, and then gives you feedback on your answers.

It's not perfect — it can't see your body language or hear your tone. But for practicing what you'll actually say? It's surprisingly effective.

## Setting Up My Mock Interview

I opened ChatGPT and gave it context about my situation. Here's the prompt I used:

prompt
I have a job interview for a Marketing Coordinator position at a mid-sized tech company. I have 4 years of experience in social media management and content creation.

Act as a hiring manager and conduct a mock interview with me. Ask me one question at a time, wait for my answer, then give me brief feedback before moving to the next question. Include a mix of behavioral questions, role-specific questions, and the classic "tell me about yourself."

Start when I say "ready."


I typed "ready" and the interview began.

## The Questions It Asked Me

The AI started with "Tell me about yourself" — classic. I gave my answer, and it pointed out that I forgot to mention why I was interested in THIS role specifically. Good catch.

Then came behavioral questions like:
- "Tell me about a time you had to meet a tight deadline"
- "Describe a situation where you disagreed with a coworker"
- "What's a campaign you're proud of and why?"

It also asked role-specific things:
- "How do you measure the success of a social media campaign?"
- "What tools do you use for content scheduling?"

## The Feedback Was Actually Useful

After each answer, the AI gave me quick notes. Things like:
- "Your answer was clear but could use a specific metric to show impact"
- "Good structure, but you took too long to get to the result"
- "Try using the STAR method here for a stronger response"

The STAR method, by the way, stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. It's a way to structure your answers so they're clear and complete.

## Practicing My Weak Spots

I noticed I kept struggling with the "weakness" question. So I asked for help:

prompt
I'm struggling with the "what's your biggest weakness" question. Give me 3 different ways to answer this authentically without sounding fake, based on my background in marketing.


It gave me options. One was about struggling with perfectionism in design work, another about initially being uncomfortable with video content. Each had a follow-up showing how I improved.

I practiced saying them out loud until one felt natural.

## The Curveball Question Round

Real interviews sometimes throw unexpected questions. I wanted to be ready:

prompt
Now ask me 5 unusual or difficult interview questions that might catch me off guard. These should be questions that require creative thinking or reveal personality. Give me one at a time.


I got questions like:
- "If you had unlimited budget for one campaign, what would you do?"
- "What would your previous manager say is your biggest area for growth?"
- "Why shouldn't we hire you?"

That last one made me pause. But practicing it meant I wouldn't freeze if something similar came up.

## Getting a Final Assessment

After about 30 minutes of back-and-forth, I asked for an overall review:

prompt
Based on our mock interview, give me a summary of my strengths, areas to improve, and 3 specific things I should practice before my real interview.


The summary was honest. It said I was good at giving concrete examples but needed to be more concise. It suggested I practice my opening and closing statements and prepare better questions to ask the interviewer.

## The Real Interview

Two days later, I walked into the actual interview. The nerves were still there, but something was different. When they asked "tell me about yourself," I had a polished answer ready. When they asked about handling tight deadlines, I had a specific story with real numbers.

I even got a curveball question about what I'd do differently in a past campaign. Because I'd practiced thinking on my feet, I didn't panic.

## The Result

I got the job. The hiring manager actually said my answers were "really well-structured." I didn't tell her I'd rehearsed with an AI for hours.

## The Takeaway

Mock interviews with AI won't replace real human practice, but they're available at 11 PM when your friends aren't. They don't judge your stumbling. They let you practice the same question five times until you get it right.

Before your next interview, spend 30 minutes doing this. Practice out loud, not just in your head. When you've already answered "tell me about yourself" ten times, the eleventh time — the real one — feels easy.
