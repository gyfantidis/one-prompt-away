## The Problem With AI Blog Posts

You know that feeling. You ask ChatGPT to write a blog post, and it spits out something that screams "a robot wrote this."

Phrases like "In today's fast-paced world" or "Let's dive in" or "It's important to note that." Dead giveaways.

I spent weeks tweaking my approach. The result? A single prompt that produces content I can publish with minimal edits.

## Why Most AI Writing Sounds Fake

The issue isn't ChatGPT. It's how we ask.

When you say "write a blog post about X," the model defaults to its most common training patterns. And those patterns are... generic.

Think about it. ChatGPT learned from millions of mediocre articles. So "write a blog post" triggers mediocre mode.

The fix? Give it a specific voice. Specific constraints. Specific examples of what NOT to do.

## The Prompt That Changed Everything

Here's what I use now:

prompt
You are a blogger who writes like they're explaining something to a smart friend over coffee. Your style:

- Short sentences. Short paragraphs.
- First person, casual tone ("I found that...", "Here's what worked...")
- No filler phrases (never use "dive in", "game-changer", "in today's world", "it's worth noting")
- Start with a problem or story, not a definition
- End sections with something actionable
- Use "you" often — talk TO the reader

Topic: [YOUR TOPIC]

Write a 600-word blog post. No intro fluff. Get to the point in the first sentence.


That's it. I paste this, add my topic, and get something usable in under a minute.

## What Makes This Prompt Work

Three things:

**1. Negative constraints matter more than positive ones.**

Telling ChatGPT what NOT to write is more effective than describing what you want. The banned phrases list eliminates 80% of the AI smell.

**2. The "smart friend" framing changes everything.**

This single metaphor shifts the entire output. No more textbook explanations. No more Wikipedia-style neutrality.

**3. Structure instructions prevent rambling.**

"Start with a problem" and "end with something actionable" give the model clear guardrails. Without these, you get five paragraphs of context before anything useful.

## My 10-Minute Workflow

Here's exactly how I use this:

**Minute 0-1:** Paste the prompt, add my topic, hit enter.

**Minute 1-3:** Read the output. Highlight anything that still sounds off.

**Minute 3-6:** Rewrite the intro myself. The intro is where human touch matters most. I keep maybe 30% of what ChatGPT wrote and add my own angle.

**Minute 6-9:** Add one personal example or opinion that only I could write. This is the authenticity anchor.

**Minute 9-10:** Quick pass for any remaining AI phrases. Search for "crucial" (delete), "landscape" (delete), "leverage" (replace with "use").

Done.

## The Phrases I Always Delete

I keep a mental list. When I see these, I know the AI slipped through:

- "In the world of..."
- "Whether you're a... or a..."
- "The key takeaway is..."
- "This is where X comes in"
- "At the end of the day"
- Any sentence starting with "Moreover" or "Furthermore"

These are the fingerprints. Remove them and your content passes the human test.

## One More Trick: The Revision Prompt

If the first output still sounds robotic, I use this follow-up:

prompt
Rewrite this but:
- Cut 30% of the words
- Remove any sentence that doesn't add new information
- Replace formal words with casual ones
- Add one slightly imperfect sentence (a fragment, a one-word paragraph, something unexpected)


That last point is counterintuitive. But perfect grammar is an AI tell. Real writers break rules on purpose.

## What You Should Do Right Now

Copy that main prompt. Save it somewhere you'll actually find it.

Next time you need a blog post, start there. Spend your editing time on the intro and adding one personal story.

The goal isn't AI-free writing. It's AI-assisted writing that sounds like you.

Ten minutes. Real results. No robot aftertaste.
